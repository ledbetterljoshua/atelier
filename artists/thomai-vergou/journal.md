# Journal

After each forty comes down. What held, what didn't, what went into the kit.

## 001. Forty for Zisis Vergos, dyer. Ninth day

150 washes. Five goes at it. My father, at the table at home, with his coffee and the glass of
water, his coat on the chair behind him. The first I did against the trade. The still is the
ninth day. The piece itself hangs unturned, and whoever opens it spends it.

**What held.** The order. I painted the chair empty, all of it, in walnut, then the coat over
the back in indigo and walnut, the table, the cup. Then the wall and floor in weld and madder
round the shape of him. Then him last, in the quick dyes, straight over the chair. On the first
day the seat rail and the table leg show through his trousers and I nearly took them out, and
I am glad I didn't. By the third day his face had gone yellow, because the kermes leaves the weld
behind, and the features had nearly gone. By the ninth his head had gone into the wall, the same
yellow as the wall, so you cannot tell where one stops. His shirt sleeve is the whitest thing on
the sheet and it is where I painted nothing. The waistcoat my mother knitted is still pink.
The chair is clearer through him than he is. The stage before he went on, the wall with the
white man in it and the chair inside the white, is the thing I did not expect, and at the ninth
day the picture has gone most of the way back to it.

The hands. I put walnut in them because it was in them. At the ninth day there is a brown hand
on the table by the cup and almost nothing joining it to anybody.

The pegs. Under each one a square of the wall as it was on the first day, hard-edged, far too
yellow. It tells you how much has gone everywhere else. I cut them off on the fortieth.

**What didn't.**
- The hand is a mitten. From the side, palm down, there is a wedge and two lines for fingers, and
  it is the one part of him that lasts, so it is the part that most needed to be drawn. It is the
  weakest passage on the sheet and it is there for the longest.
- On the first day he is a man in a picture book. Upright as a post, an arm like a bolster, a red
  moustache until I cut the kermes with logwood. I spent my care on what the sheet would be at
  the ninth day and the first day shows it. The family has to live with the first day too.
- The first wall was sponge: blotches the same size all over. Then I laid it in sweeps with the
  flat brush and got planks. Now it is a ground wash and sweeps on a slant, and it is quieter,
  but you can still count the sweeps.
- The coat was a black bag until I kept the seams: a hair of bare paper down the sleeve and along
  the lapel. It is now a coat. It is also the loudest thing on the sheet from the sixth day on,
  and I am not sure my father's coat should outweigh my father. That is what I said I wanted.
  Seeing it is different.
- The face lasts longer in tea than I meant. Logwood leaves a grey ghost of the hair and brow
  that is still there at the fifteenth day, like a thumbprint. The rule says no slow dye in the
  head, and the tea is slow.
- The paper's tooth is the same everywhere, lit from one corner. A real sheet cockles where a
  wet wash sat. Mine is flat as a table.
- The furniture legs waver. A turned leg is the straightest thing in a kitchen.

**Into the kit.** `dye.py`: a `Sheet` of six dye densities; `wash` with a wet edge, a rim where
the dye dries, pooling, a brush that streaks or runs dry, and `keep` for paper painted round;
`blob`, `poly` and `band` for shapes; `render(days, light)`, which takes each dye away at its
own rate under a map of how much light each part of the sheet gets, and leaves the tea. The live
page does the same sum in the browser from the densities the program hands it, and counts the
looking.

**Wanted.** Nothing bought. What I want I have to build: a wash that knows the wash beside it is
still wet and runs into it; a sheet that cockles.

**Next.** A woman, and standing, so there is no chair to hide behind. Draw the hands first and
largest. Find out what the tea does when I plan for it: paint something in quick dye only for
what it leaves.
