# Thomaí Vergou

b. 1958, Kastoria. Her Kastoria is in the world of the Clause, described below, where an image
may be made but not kept. Daughter of a fur dyer. Worked from 1975 to 2003 in the colour room of a fur-dyeing
works on the Dolcho shore, testing batches for fastness: a strip of each dyed pelt went in the
window for a month and she wrote down what was left. Painted forties from 1989, at first in the
trade manner, for neighbours, in the evenings. Her own way from 2004, the year her father died.
She has painted about three hundred. None exists.

## What she makes

Forties: the mourning likeness that hangs by the window for forty days and is white at the end.
She paints the dead person in their own kitchen, sitting where they sat, with what was on the
table. The trade paints the face in the slowest dye, so that it keeps to the last. She does the
opposite. The face goes on in kermes, the quickest dye sold, and is gone by the memorial of the
third day. What she puts on in walnut and indigo, the slow dyes, are the things the family will
still have when the forty days are over: the chair, the coat, the cup.

> "You lose the face first. Everyone does, inside the week, and then they look at the picture to
> get it back and what they get back is the picture. My grandmother's forty had her face on it
> to the last day, on white paper, with nothing round it. I was six. I can still see that and I
> cannot see her. So I let the face go when it goes. By the ninth day the picture and the house
> should look the same."

## How she works

- **Six dyes, bought by their life.** Kermes, a day and a half. Logwood, three days. Madder, four
  and a half. Weld, a week. Indigo, eleven days. Walnut, fifteen. These are the dyes the fur trade
  throws out for running; she knows each one's going from twenty-eight years of window strips.
  She does not mix a colour for how it looks on the first day without working out what it is on
  the ninth.
- **Nothing opaque, so no white.** White is the paper. A white shirt, a cup, the sun on a wall
  are places she did not paint.
- **Slow things first, whole.** The chair is painted empty and complete, the coat on its back,
  the table laid. Then the person is painted over them. On the first day the chair shows a little
  through him. That is correct, and it is how he is sitting.
- **Quick things go round him.** The wall and the floor are in quick dye and would stain a white
  shirt, so they are painted round his shape. When he has gone his shape is still there in the
  wall for some days, white, with the chair in it. She did not plan that the first time. She
  keeps it.
- **The face in kermes, with as little logwood as will make a dark.** No slow dye is allowed in
  the head.
- **One exception, if it is true.** If something slow was really in the person, it goes on in
  slow dye. Her father's hands were walnut to the wrist for fifty years.
- **Two pegs and a string, by the window, window side to the left.** She paints the room so the
  painted light and the real light come from the same side. The side nearer the glass goes
  sooner. Under the pegs it does not go at all, and on the fortieth day she cuts those two
  corners off and burns them, which is the law.
- **The title is the name, the trade, and the day she is showing it at.**
- **She will not paint from an envelope.** If she did not know the kitchen she goes and sits in
  it first.
