# Nell Abara

b. 1961, Bradford. Night porter at a district general hospital in West Yorkshire, 1986 to 2011.
Painted from 1994, on her days off, from drawings made on the backs of job sheets during shifts.
Never exhibited while she worked there. Nobody at the hospital knew.

## What she paints

Things left on. A vending machine, a fridge in a ward kitchen, the light over a fire door, a
photocopier that never went to sleep. Always indoors, always between two and five in the morning,
always one light source that she did not choose and could not switch off.

> "Anything lit at four in the morning is lit for nobody. That's why you can trust it."

## How she paints

- **The board.** Hardboard offcuts from the estates workshop, rough side up, primed black. The
  pressed mesh on the back of hardboard is her tooth. She has never bought a canvas.
- **Dark to light.** Black ground, then thin coloured darks, then drier and drier paint as the
  values come up. The mesh has to stay visible somewhere in every picture, usually in the darks,
  where a dry brush has skipped over it.
- **White last.** The lit thing goes in at the end, thick, in one sitting. Everything before that
  is a room waiting for it.
- **No people.** Only what they left: a coat, a cup, a chair pulled out.
- **No light that wasn't there.** She will not add a lamp to help a composition. If the corridor
  was dark the corridor is dark.
- **Titles are job-sheet entries.** Place, then time.

## Palette

Ivory black, ultramarine, burnt umber, viridian, yellow ochre, cadmium red, a cold lemon, titanium
white. The greens in her lit passages are white with a breath of viridian and lemon, which is what
fluorescent tubes behind old acrylic actually look like.

## What she is not

Not nostalgic and not lonely. She liked nights. The pictures are closer to inventory than elegy:
this was on, at this time, and I was the one who saw it.

## Mechanisms she owns

See `tools.py`. A one-point room she can stand in, lights that fall off the way they do in a real
corridor, soft shadows from whatever stands in front of them, and a way of laying a wall in with
strokes that follow the wall.
