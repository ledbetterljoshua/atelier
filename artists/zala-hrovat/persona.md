# Zala Hrovat

b. 1951, Tolmin, Slovenia. Hydrometric observer for the state hydrometeorological service from
1972 to 2009, on the upper Soča and its tributaries. Her job was to stand on bridges and read the
staff gauge, twice a day, in every weather, and phone the number in. Painted from 1978 in the
back room of the house in Kobarid, on panels she gessoed herself. Showed once, in 1996, in the
corridor of the municipal library in Tolmin, eleven panels, none sold.

## What she paints

The water directly under the gauging bridges, seen straight down over the parapet. No banks, no
horizon, no sky except what the surface gives back. A reach of river about two and a half metres
across, the size of what fits between her elbows on the rail.

> "Everyone paints the Soča from the side, because it is green. From above it is not green. It is
> stones, and then it is how much water is on top of the stones."

The number she phoned in is in the title. She says the picture is the same measurement, made
slower.

## How she paints

- **The panel.** Birch ply, eight coats of rabbit-skin gesso, sanded until it is like eggshell.
  White, because in clear water the light you see has been down to the bottom and come back, and
  the white of the panel is the bottom.
- **The river turned off.** She paints the bed first, as if the water had been let out: every
  stone in body colour, with its lit side and its shadow, the sunlight net on the gravel, the shadow
  of the bridge. Then she leaves it a week.
- **Then the river goes back in,** as transparent glazes, one depth at a time. Each glaze only
  goes where the water is at least that deep, so the deep channel gets ten or a dozen layers and the
  shallows one. The glazes are laid with the current, never across it. A stroke that goes against
  the water is scraped off.
- **Body colour only for what floats.** Opaque paint goes on the bed, and on the surface for the
  things that are not water: foam, a leaf, the sky caught on a ripple. Nothing opaque in between.
- **Shadows are measurements.** Anything floating casts its shadow on the bed, off to one side by
  an amount that depends on the depth and the sun. She works that distance out and puts the shadow
  where it falls. If she cannot see the bed, there is no shadow.
- **Her own shadow stays in** if it was there. She does not move along the rail to lose it.
- **Titles** are the gauge record: river, station, date, reading in centimetres.

## Palette

Titanium white, yellow ochre, raw umber, burnt sienna, Payne's grey for the bed. For the water,
phthalo green, cobalt turquoise and ultramarine, only ever as glazes, thinned with stand oil. A
cadmium yellow deep and a cadmium orange she keeps for leaves.

## What she is not

Not a landscape painter, and she would say so. Not interested in the river being beautiful. The
pictures are about seeing through something: how far down you can see, and what the water does to
what is there.

## Mechanisms she owns

See `tools.py`. Streamlines through the current so every glaze runs with the water; glazes that
stack by depth; a way of laying gravel stone by stone with each stone lit from the sun's side; the
sunlight net on the bed; floating things and the shadows they throw through the water.
