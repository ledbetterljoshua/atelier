# Journal

Notes after each light. What the reckoning got right, what it got wrong, what I would keep.

## 001. Brask wall, east run. Twelfth light, for Ione Brask, breast-high

Twelve lights now in the east run, eleven of them Juno's. Ione Brask scraped that keep for most of
her life with the pan held against her chest, so breast-high, 1.3 off the floor, bore 0.29, pointed
at the middle of the sway. I put it in the one wide stretch of clean hoar Juno left, between the
low light at the west of it and the narrow one to the east. Cost said to the house before the iron
went in: 54.5 square metres of a keep of 2,390, one part in forty-four. They said yes.

What the gallery has is the reckoning itself, run as a view from the ward mast. It starts at the
boring, with the sun's patch lying on hoar that has not yet given way, and the eye opens over six
sways. One sway takes two minutes. The still is the first sway, at the bottom of the sun's loop,
when every patch on the keep is at its longest.

**What held.** The eye is not drawn anywhere. The program has a sun that leans round its loop, a
wall with a top, and round holes in it, and for each patch of floor it adds up what share of the
sway that patch can see any of the sun. Hoar stands where the share is next to nothing. The eyes
came out of that, narrow at the wall and wide at the far end, all leaning the same way because the
sway leans, and each with an island of hoar left in the middle where the patch goes round but never
crosses. I did not know about the island until the reckoning showed it. Juno's eleven have it; I
have looked at them from the mast all my life and never asked why.

Raising the wall in the reckoning from 5.2 to 6.8, which is what the Brask wall is, changed the
picture more than anything else. At 5.2 the keep ended in the middle of the eyes and the whole
thing read as a torn fringe. At the true height there is a field, and the eyes are in it.

The first sway is the right moment. Eleven patches lie down the dark of their own eyes on wet
gravel, and one lies on white. That is the only way to tell which light is new.

Her way in is there too: along the foot of the wall from the east end, the trodden place under the
bore, the core tipped out. It rimes over as the eye opens.

**What didn't.**
- The twelfth is not the loudest thing. At the low end of the sway the patch through the pair,
  west of it, is as bright and longer. I moved the still round the sway looking for a place where
  the new one wins, and where it does (high sun, a short hot lozenge) the other eleven go quiet
  and the picture stops saying what it is. I took the one that tells it.
- The walls are slabs. Ours has courses and rime at the foot and nothing else; the town's have
  less. I grew up ramming these. There are shutter marks, lifts that did not key, patches of a
  different gravel, the stain under every old bore. None of it is here. This is the weakest
  passage, and it is right across the middle.
- The sky is bars of haze made out of noise and it looks like it. The horizon has nothing on it.
- Hoar is the same grain everywhere. It grows as needles into the wind, and from the mast the near
  hoar should show which way nightward is. It is speckle.
- How much sun the hoar will stand is a number I chose by eye until the old eyes closed at their
  far ends the way they do from the mast. So the reckoning is fitted to Juno's lights. It has not
  yet been right about one of mine. Ask me at the sixth sway.
- A bore is reckoned as one round opening that narrows as you move off its line. Good enough for
  the wide ones. The narrow light Juno aimed high should cut an arc, and the reckoning gives a
  smudge.
- The glow round each patch, on the hoar beside it, is a guess.
- In the first still, with no wall, there is a seam in the bare ground where the wall will go.
- The live piece is reckoned from nothing every frame, so it runs small, 480 across, about seven
  frames a second. It is a slow thing and can stand it, but dragging the sun round is stiff.
- The footprints read as a dashed line and not as someone walking.

**Into the kit.** Nothing moved out of the work yet. If the next one stands on the same reckoning,
these go to my folder: `sunAt` (where the sun is in its loop), `throughBore` (how much of the disc
a patch of floor sees down one bore), `overWall` (the same over the top and round the end),
`reckon` (the share of the sway, added up over the keep).

**Wanted.** Nothing this time.

**Next.** One light, close, from the foot of the wall: the kept mouth of a bore and the rammed
face it is cut through, with the patch at my feet. I have to be able to make a wall before I am
allowed another field of them. And I want the narrow bore reckoned as what it is, two mouths 2.2
apart, so that an arc is an arc.
