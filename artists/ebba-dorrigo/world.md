# Low Kessim, on the dusk ring of Sennet

Sennet turns once for each time it goes round its sun, so the sun does not rise or set. At Low
Kessim it stands a hand above the sunward horizon and stays there. People landed 32,771 sways ago,
which is a little over two thousand of Earth's years; nobody in the town keeps Earth's count, and
by it this is somewhere in the fifty-fifth century. About four thousand people live on the dry
shelf above the meltwater, in wards of long walls.

## What is true there

- **The sun leans, and that is all it does.** Over one sway, twenty-three of Earth's days, it goes
  once round a small tilted loop: up and down by a little over two of its own widths, side to
  side by five and a half. A sway is the year. There is no day, no night and no season. People sleep by the ward
  bell.
- **A shadow is a place.** It has been where it is since the thing that throws it was built, and
  it has an owner. Nobody has watched a shadow cross a floor during a meal.
- **The wind comes from nightward,** always, cold, carrying ice too fine to see. Wherever the sun
  never comes, hoar grows on the ground. Wherever it comes for even a small share of the sway,
  the ground is bare gravel.
- **Water is scraped.** Each household rams a shade wall across the light and owns the keep behind
  it: the strip of ground the wall hides from the whole sway. The hoar on a keep is the house's
  water. A keep ends where the top of the wall stops hiding the sun at the top of the sway.
- **Light has one colour and comes from one side.** Anything lit is orange going to white. Blue is
  only ever seen on unlit things, and the word for blue and the word for kept are the same word.

## What that does to looking

On the level, everything is edge-on to the light, so the ground is streaks and glare. To see what
is on a keep you climb. Every ward has a mast, built so the wardens can count hoar, and the view
from the mast is the only view of a keep that anyone calls looking at it.

The sway is too slow to watch. A patch of sun on the ground does not visibly move. The one thing
that shows a whole sway at once is hoar, because hoar adds it up: it stands where the sun has not
been at any time in the sway, and is gone where it has. So a mark in hoar is the shape of a year,
and nothing else there is.

## What that does to making

There are no pictures to hang. A wall's kept face is dark and its other face is glare. What the
town has instead is the light: a round hole bored through a shade wall. The sun comes through it
and lies on the keep as a patch, the patch goes round with the sway, and where it goes the hoar
dies back to gravel in a long closed ring, narrow at the wall and wide at the far end. They call
the ring an eye.

A light is bored for one dead person, by their house, through the house's own wall. It costs the
house water for as long as the wall stands, so the ward agrees its size, and the house agrees to
it out loud. The borer does not choose who. The borer chooses where along the wall, how high, how
wide, and where in the sway the bore is pointed, and those four choices are the whole of the art:
a low light makes a small sharp eye at the foot of the wall, a high one a long faint eye that may
run out past the keep and only bite its edge, and a narrow bore through a thick wall sees only the
part of the sway it is aimed at and cuts an arc.

A borer cannot see the work while making it. A new light lies on clean hoar and looks like
nothing. The eye opens over five or six sways. It cannot be corrected. It can be stopped with a
plug of rammed gravel, and a stopped light is a shame on the house and on the borer both.

## What that does to showing

At the sixth sway the house climbs the ward mast and reads the eye. That is the showing. After
that it is there for anyone who climbs, for as long as the wall is kept. Nobody walks a keep, to
look or for any other reason; a footprint is water.

Nobody sees the work whole in one look either. From the mast you see the eye, which is everywhere
the sun has been, and somewhere on it the patch, which is where the sun is.
