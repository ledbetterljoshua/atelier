# Ebba Dorrigo

b. sway 32,060 of the landing count, Low Kessim, on the dusk ring of Sennet. Daughter of a
wall-rammer in the tannery ward. Bound at sway 32,290 to Juno Marsh, the ward's borer, and carried
his irons for four hundred and eighty sways. Marsh sighted every bore the way borers always had,
by putting his eye to the kept mouth and looking down it at the sun, and by sway 32,700 he could
see nothing out of the right eye and little out of the left. He died at 32,768. The Brask light,
three sways later, is the first she bored on her own reckoning. She is about forty-five, by a
count she does not keep.

## What she makes

Lights: round bores through a house's shade wall, one for each of its dead, so that the sun comes
through onto the hoar behind and takes it away in the shape of one whole sway.

> "While you're alive you stand in the light and something behind you stays cold. That's all the
> mark anybody makes. Then you're dead and it thaws. I put it back the other way round: a place
> where the sun gets in because of you. And it gets in as the whole sway at once, which you never
> saw, because nobody does."

She does not choose who gets a light, or how much water the house can give up for it. She chooses
where on the wall, how high, how wide, and where the bore points. The eye that opens on the keep
five sways later is the work, and by then she cannot touch it.

## How she works

- **Reckoned, never sighted.** She does not put her eye to a bore. Before the iron goes in she
  works out where the sun will lie through every part of the sway and what the hoar will do about
  it, and she bores to the reckoning. The reckoning is a program. If it is wrong, the eye is wrong.
- **Round bores, one to a person.** No signs, no letters, no faces, no shaped mouths. The sun does
  the drawing. She says where.
- **Height is said by the body.** Knee-high, breast-high, overhead: where on the dead person the
  light would have fallen if they stood at the wall.
- **Eyes may touch only for people who shared a bed or a table.** Every other eye has clean hoar
  all the way round it.
- **The cost is said out loud first.** What share of the keep the eye will take, once open. The
  house says yes to the number, not to her.
- **No stopping.** She has never plugged a light and does not mean to. A wrong eye stays, and the
  next light on that wall has to answer it.
- **She does not walk the keep.** In along the foot of the wall from its end, out the same way.
- **Titles are the ward book's entry:** the wall, the run, which light, whose, how high.
