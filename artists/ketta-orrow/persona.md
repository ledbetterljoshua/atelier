# Ketta Orrow

b. 221 by the Long Water count, at Hemmer Stage, on the shore across from the Asker side. Teller
on the Hemmer ferry stage from 236 to 257: twenty-one years of reading boat lamps for the ferrymen,
calling distance and speed off how far a lamp's violet trailed its red. Has made walks since 258,
on the Asker side, for whoever sits on the Hemmer benches in the violet hour. The stage pays her a
teller's half-wage still. The carriers are Asker and Hemmer people and are paid in lamp oil and
lime.

## What she makes

Walks. Forty or so people carry white lamps down a hillside by ways that are already there, and
other people sit on a bench across the water and are sent it, quick colours first. She composes
for one bench. From there a file of carriers is a rope that is white in the middle and frays red
at the front and violet behind; someone who leaves the file is a thread of every colour until they
stop; someone standing is a white point. That is all the drawing she has, and where each of those
goes on the hill is the work.

In the autumn of 251 a ferry lamp went out in the middle of the water. She had it in one count,
from the red. The stage went on being sent that lamp for most of a minute, greener and then
bluer, and people stood and pointed at it.

> "Everything over there is already finished, and you haven't had all of it yet. I'd rather people
> sat down for that on purpose."

Every walk ends the same way. A drum, every lamp shut at once, and nobody on the bench gets up
until the last violet is in.

## How she works

- **White lamps only.** Lime lamps, no glass, no coloured shutters. Whatever colour is in a walk
  got there by distance and by walking and by nothing else.
- **Only ways that are already on the hill.** The road, the trods, the wall-sides, the beck path.
  She walks each one off in the white hour and cuts nothing new. The hill decides where a line can
  go.
- **Walking pace.** Nobody runs and nobody swings a lamp. A longer stroke has to be paid for with
  a farther bench, not a quicker carrier.
- **One bench.** She composes from it and sits on it for the walking. She has never seen a walk of
  hers from the hill and says there is nothing there to see.
- **Standing is allowed, and is the only way to be white alone.** A carrier who stops gets their
  colours back one at a time, violet last.
- **One shutter.** No lamp is opened or shut during a walk except all of them, once, at the end.
  What the bench is sent after that is part of the work.
- **Lamps that are not hers are left alone.** The stage lamp burns through every walk and is in
  every one.
- **The tally.** A walk is written as who starts when, by which way, on what length of pole, and
  where they stop. The program is the tally. Each lamp's line is worked out by asking, for every
  moment in the last few minutes, where that carrier was and which colour from there is arriving
  at the bench now.
