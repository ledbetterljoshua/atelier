# Journal

Notes after each walk. What the bench was sent, what it should have been sent, and what goes into
the tally next time.

## 001. Asker road, forty down and one along the top

Forty-two lamps. Forty in file down the Asker road from the pass to the quay, six turns, about half
an hour. One of the forty leaves at the second turn for Hask farm and stands at the door; one stops
at the gate on the fourth leg; one leaves at the fifth turn for the boathouse. A late one comes
over the pass by himself when the file is already halfway down and goes along the top to the cairn
instead. One starts at the water, comes up the beck trod, and meets the last of the file where the
road crosses. Shore bench, five hundred paces. The still is count 945.

**What held.** The poles. First time through I had every lamp at one height and the file came out
as a white tube with a glow round it, a thing you could buy. Poles of different lengths, a pace and
a half to three and a half, and the file is forty threads lying one over another, each its own
colour at any place on the road because each carrier passed that place at a different count. It is
white only where enough of them cross. I did not expect the white to be woven and it is the best
thing in the walk.

The turn. At count 945 the file is bent round the third turn, so the bench is sent its front on
the lower leg and its back on the upper one: red, orange and yellow below, blue and violet above,
and all of it pulled tight and white at the bend, where everybody's every colour is in the same
few paces. I chose the count for that. A file on a straight leg is a rope. A file round a turn is
open like a hand.

The late one along the top. A single lamp against the sky makes the thinnest line there is, and at
945 he has reached the cairn and stopped, so he is a white point with the rest of himself still
coming along the ridge behind him, green nearest, violet a hundred and eighty paces back. He is what the whole
walk is about and he is one thread.

After the shutter is better than I planned. I had thought of it as everything going violet
together. It doesn't. The quay is near and is violet inside a minute; Hask farm is still blue; the
cairn, farthest, is still green. The lamps go out in order of how far away they are and you can
read the hill's depth off it.

**What didn't.**
- The hill is the weakest passage. It is a smooth slope with walls drawn on it. I tried trees
  twice, first as blocks and then as domes, and both times they looked like things set down on a
  table, so the Asker side has gorse and no trees and is barer than it should be. The left third,
  past Hask, has nothing in it but a farm lamp. The walls are all one width and one tone, like a
  pen line.
- The water. The file's reflection was the loudest thing in the picture for three passes: a
  coloured slab, then a coloured blanket. It is broken into dashes now and is quieter than the file,
  but the dashes are too even, and the lower edge of the frame cuts it off before it has finished.
- The light on the ground under the file is a twenty-fifth of what forty lamps would throw. At the
  true amount the grass along the road lit up green and the file looked like a tube again. I held
  it down by hand. That is a decision and I should say so.
- Every bench is given the same view. Moving back from the shore bench to the top bench makes the
  strokes longer, which is right, and leaves the hill the same size, which is not.
- The live piece goes at eight times walking pace, or nobody here would stay. At walking pace the
  strokes hardly seem to move; you look away and look back and the red is further on. That is the
  actual walk and it is not what the page shows. The flames flicker eight times too quick as well.
- The red end is short. How slow each colour is between red and violet is one curve with one
  number in it (`DISP`) that I set by eye until the head of a stroke was not all rainbow. I have no
  better reason for it.
- A lamp behind a house or a fold is hidden, which is right, but its ground light is not, and
  nothing casts a shadow in lamp light.
- The drum. Sound is quicker than red there: the bench would hear the shutter-drum before it saw
  anything change. The piece is silent.

**Into the kit.** Nothing lifted out yet; one walk is not a kit. What is in `piece/walk.js` that a
second walk would want: the hill as ground you can ask (`bare`, `ask` for the nearest road, wall,
trod, beck), `way` for a walked line with rounded turns, `where` for a carrier's place at a count,
and the loop in `draw` that goes back through every moment whose light is arriving now and works
out which colour it is. `make.mjs` writes the stills from the same file the page runs.

**Wanted.** Nothing that needs a package. Trees that stand up. Sound from the page itself: the
drum, arriving early.

**Next.** A walk with no file in it. Forty people each alone on forty different ways, so that from
the shore bench there is no white anywhere until they stop, and then compose the stopping: who gets
their colours back first, and where on the hill the white comes on. Shorter, from a farther bench.
And give the far benches their own view.
