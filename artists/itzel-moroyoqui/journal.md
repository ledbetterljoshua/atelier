# Journal

After each panel. What held, what didn't, what went into the kit.

## 001. Calle Yáñez, 46°

2,697 strokes. About a dozen goes, and the first five were a beach.

**What held.** The scallops. The awning's shade comes to an edge that is a row of little arches,
one for each corrugation of the lámina, and I didn't draw them: I asked each point of the pavement
whether the lámina stood between it and the sun, and the arches are where the answer changes. The
edge then went on once with the lettering brush from the shade side, and the shade is darkest and
most violet right there, which is how it looks when you are sitting in it. The same edge crosses the
back of my box, so the lid is half hot orange and half in the shade with the arches across it. That
was an accident of where I put the box and it is the best thing in the panel.

The garrafón works. Its shadow is the blue one, lighter than the awning's because the water lets
most of the sun through, and in the middle of it is a long white mark where the water has gathered
the sun and put it down again. That is the only pure white I used apart from two glints, and it is
the brightest thing on the board. The rule held: the sunlit pavement is the palest cream and yellow
I could mix and it still reads as blazing next to the shade, because it is not white.

Light first, all of it, with no pickup and nothing running together, then the shade over it wet.
It works the way acrylic in the heat works. Where the shade paint ran thin the light shows through
in warm threads, and in the end I wanted fewer of those, not more.

**What didn't.**
- The first five goes were the sea at sunset: a turquoise band at the top, a violet band that went
  pink toward the bottom, and sand. A gradient laid in horizontal strokes is a sky whatever you meant
  by it. What made it a pavement was the ground itself: the saw-cut joints running away from me, a
  broom finish across the concrete in hairlines, the yellow kerb and the street at the bottom, three
  mesquite pods and a bottle cap lying flat. Things lying on the ground told the eye it was ground.
- The warm band along the edge of the shade, where the lit pavement bounces into it, is true and kept
  reading as afterglow. I cut it back to a narrow strip.
- Strokes laid across the shade in short dabs came out as a leopard. Strokes laid along the ground
  away from me came out as rain. What covers is long strokes laid right across the shape, edge to
  edge, with the tape making their ends.
- A brush picking up over a light ground drags pale streaks through everything: the wall was a
  curtain with the primer showing through in stripes for four goes. First coat with no pickup, then
  wet into it.
- I had the sun behind me first, so the jug and the box hid their own shadows. Moved it round to the
  right. At ten to two in August it would be higher than this. I chose the shadow over the clock.
- A long thin stroke carrying a little paint runs dry halfway across the board: the joints stopped in
  the middle of nowhere until I gave them more.

**Still wrong.** The jug is the weakest passage. It is too smooth: thirty-odd strokes mixed for
every place they land and it comes out looking turned on a lathe, not painted on my knees. The cap
is a dark smudge. The open door is a dark slab with shelves you can only find if you know they are
there. The middle of the shade is a large soft field of violet that does nothing much; it is meant to
be the room I am sitting in and it is mostly a colour. The kerb is the loudest yellow in the picture
and pulls the eye to the bottom edge.

**Into the kit.** `plywood`; `View`, standing on the pavement and looking down, with a ray through
every pixel so I can ask what each one sees; `ray_box`; `sweep`, long strokes edge to edge across a
shape on the board, mixed along their length, each varied a little from the last; `lay_ground` and
`sweep_ground`, strokes placed and turned in metres on the pavement so they lie down with distance.

**Asked of the engine.**
- A stroke that carries the same load wherever it starts. A path that begins off the board spends its
  paint on nothing before it arrives; I clip my own lines to the board, and every painter must
  be doing the same.
- Not an ask, a confirmation of Gavino's note: a coat over the white primer only half covers per
  stroke, the next stroke in the same pass picks that half-covered mix up, and it streaks pale. On a
  light ground the first coat goes on with pickup at zero.

**Next.** Under a mesquite, where the shade is not one shape but a hundred, each hole in the leaves a
little picture of the sun. And something alive in there with me: the dog that is always under that
tree.
