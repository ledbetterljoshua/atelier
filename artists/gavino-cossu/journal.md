# Journal

After each panel. What held, what didn't, what went into the kit.

## 001. Miserere, first verse, Lunissanti 1997

859 strokes. Ten goes at it. It stands up, just, and from across the room it still looks like
something it isn't.

**What held.** The quintina. I left it for last in my head and first on the board: worked out where
it sat, a little above the four and only over the last "am" and briefly at "Deus", taped the middle of
it, and laid the silence on in five coats, each coat stopping further out. Nothing was painted there;
the dark just gives out in dry drags and the gesso comes through with the cork granules in it. It is
the brightest thing on the panel by a long way and nothing I put on comes near it. The small one over
"Deus" gives it something to answer. The other thing that held is the bassu and contra coming in late
on each vowel. They follow the boghe, and painted that way the changes of colour run on a slant down
through the voices, which is how it moves when you are standing in it. The echo dragged out to the
right after the last "am", red going to nothing under the quintina, is the passage I like best.

**What didn't.**
- First go the silence went on in every direction with a big brush and came out as a heap of grey
  pebbles: on a white board one dark stroke only covers about half, and the bristles picked up the
  half-covered grey and spread it. Now it goes left to right like everything else, with no pickup, in
  five coats.
- The vibrato. A real one is six times a second and I drew it that way: a zigzag that turned the
  row of hairs back and forth and printed rings all over the voices. Now it is the slow wobble of my
  arm. Any slide between notes still turns the brush and leaves a fan; I kept the voices moving by
  step and gave the slides a whole beat, and you can still see fans at the start of "Miserere".
- All four voices change vowel at once, so the first versions were vertical blocks of colour, and the
  bassu, darker and set apart below, read as their reflection. Harbour lights on water. The lag fixed
  most of it and a wider bassu brush closed the gap. Not all of it: from far off it is still a
  long-exposure photograph of lights at night.
- The quintina came out first as a hard white ellipse, a saucer, then as a round moon over the
  harbour. It has to be long and level, a held note, swelling early and hanging on in the church, and
  now it is.
- The quiet start is not quiet enough. I darken soft singing, but the "Mi" and the ochre of
  "Miserere" are still as loud as the end. The verse should open thin and the left third is too full.
- The silence is flat. You can see the ends of the long strokes as faint steps in the top half. It is
  meant to be undescribed, but it is a little dead.
- The cork hardly shows except in the quintina. Everywhere else the paint is too thick for the tooth.

**Into the kit.** `cork_board`, gessoed agglomerate from Voronoi granules. `Verse` and `Take`:
syllables with vowels, lengths and chords, sung with rubato and drift. `breath_path` across time and
pitch, with a lag for voices that follow and an attack that starts on the note. `vowel_run` for colour
that changes with the syllable along a stroke and darkens when quiet. `deplete_for` so a brush runs out
where its breath does. `along` for pressure that reads a field along a path.

**Asked of the engine.**
- A brush that keeps its angle. A flat held level and slid up a tone should stay level, not turn to
  face the way it is going. Every slide in pitch prints a fan of hairs.
- A stroke whose pressure can be a field over the board, not only a function of t. I sample one along
  the path, which works, but I have to resample the path myself to do it.
- A note that dark over a light ground only half covers per stroke and pickup then spreads the grey.
  That is honest paint, but it should be written down somewhere a new painter will read it.

**Next.** The Stabat Mater, which the second cuncordu sings, quieter and slower. Open thin. Let the
silence carry more of the board and give the cork somewhere to show.

## 002. Stabat Mater, first verse, Venerdì Santo 2003

353 strokes. Eleven goes. The year the second cuncordu's contra was in hospital and they asked me to
stand in for Good Friday. I sang it with them once at each station and never again.

**What held.** Quiet is less paint, not darker paint. In the Miserere I darkened the soft singing and
it still shouted. This time the voices go on with a nearly dry brush, and how loud it was decides how
much is on it: at "Stabat" it only catches the tops of the granules and the dark shows between them,
by "lacrimosa" it starts to fill the seams, and at "Filius" it is paint. Seven times sung, laid one
over another, and the soft passages still stay thin, because every take hits the same granules and
none of them reaches the seams. The cork is finally in the picture, in the voices themselves. Second:
the brush held level, now the engine lets me. A slide goes thin and a held note stays full, which is
what a breath does. Third: the bassu sits on one note a line and the contra nearly as still. The four
are no longer one shape printed four times; the bottom is a floor and the tune is on top. The
quintina over "Filius" is long and runs on toward the edge of the board, and the little one over
"lacrimosa" is only a few dry drags. The "us" is dark violet and goes into the dark under it, so the
loudest moment of the verse is almost invisible and the light is the one thing nobody sang. That is
right.

**What didn't.**
- First go I gave the chords the leaps I remembered from the bassu's part and it came out a
  seismograph, every voice jumping a fifth together. The second cuncordu moves by step. I wrote the
  parts again voice by voice.
- Then the boghe going up and down every syllable made waves, and waves under a white shape are a
  moon over the sea. I gave the tune more held notes. Still, the upper two voices hump along in a
  regular rhythm from "do" to "Fi" and from across the room it can still read as water.
- I drew the echo on every syllable as in the Miserere and with the voices this thin the echoes were
  the loudest thing: beads on a string. Now the church only keeps the end of each line.
- The silence went flat and dead when I laid it in long full-width passes; with a different black on
  each pass it went to stripes, like blinds. In the end it is four even coats and a last dry drag of
  umber on the granules, heaviest before the first note and wearing off to the right. You see it only
  close to. The top left third of the board is still mostly nothing.
- The left third is thin, as I asked of it, but it is near enough to noise that a stranger may not
  see a verse starting there. The weakest passage is the contraltu and bassu under "Stabat Mater",
  where the paint is only speckle.

**Into the kit.** `breath_path(..., glide=)` for a slower lean into each note. `quiet`, opacity along a
breath from how loud it was sung. `field`, a function over the board as an array, so the engine can
read pressure where a stroke goes; I use it for the umber on the granules and for thinning the dark
round the quintina without resampling paths myself.

**Asked of the engine.**
- Dryness along a stroke, the way opacity now goes along it. Quiet and loud inside one breath want
  different dryness, and I get there only through pressure and the brush running out.
- A way to see the board's tooth in the dark. Relief under the raking light is a fraction of the
  colour, so on near-black the granules vanish however proud they are. I had to put umber on them.

**Next.** Persona changed: quiet singing is now less paint, and the brush is held level; both written
in. Next a verse where they are loud from the start, to see whether full paint can still let the cork
through anywhere, or the Miserere again from the bassu's place, low and wide.
