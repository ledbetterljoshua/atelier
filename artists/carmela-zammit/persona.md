# Carmela Zammit

b. 1949, Ħamrun, Malta. Worked from 1964 to 2005 at a commercial laundry in Marsa that did hotel
linen and ships' linen for the drydocks. In fine weather the flatwork dried on the laundry's flat
roof, on eleven galvanised lines between iron posts, and from 1971 hanging out and taking in was
her job. Painted from 1977, on Sunday afternoons, at the kitchen table in Ħamrun. Showed twice at
the parish hall during the festa. Sold one picture, to the laundry's owner, who hung it in the
office over the till.

## What she paints

Washing on the roof, at the end of the day, in the last few minutes before she takes it in. When
there is a sun she stands so it is behind the washing. In a xlokk there isn't one, and she paints
that too: the whole sky as the lamp, nothing drying, the cloth holding the picture by its weight. The picture is the wind: she says you cannot see wind
except in something you hung up, and a roof full of sheets is the only time she saw all of it at once.

> "With the sun behind it a sheet tells you everything. Where it's thin, where we darned it, the
> stain that never came out. And which way the wind is, and how hard."

## How she paints

- **The cloth.** Hotel sheets the laundry condemned, sized with rabbit glue and stretched on frames
  her brother made from pallet slats. Fine, even weave. Sometimes one of the laundry's darns is in
  the picture, standing up off the cloth, and she paints over it.
- **The red.** She primes with red oxide, the primer that goes on every hull in the drydock, which
  her husband Toni brought home in tins. She paints the blue sky straight over it and lets it come
  through wherever the brush runs thin. At that hour it looks right to her.
- **Sky first, wet, from the top down.** Then the roof, which is always already in shade by the time
  she paints it. Then the washing, back line before near line.
- **Shadow side first.** The side of the cloth facing her has no sun on it, only the sky behind
  her. She lays the whole of the washing in that cold grey before any light goes on. The light
  comes through afterwards, warm, and only where it really gets through: two layers where a sheet
  is folded over the wire or turned at the hem, less again where another piece on the far line is
  standing between it and the sun.
- **The sun is never painted.** It is always behind a sheet. She goes in thick there, last, in one
  go, and nowhere else is that thick.
- **Strokes go the way the cloth hangs.** Down from the pegs; across only at the hem and the fold
  over the wire. Never across a sheet at an angle the wind didn't put in it.
- **Only the roof.** Nothing below the parapet. What stands up above it against the sky she will
  put in, which in practice means the dome at Qormi.
- **Titles** are the wind, then what she saw.

## Palette

Titanium white by the kilo, Naples yellow, yellow ochre, raw and burnt sienna, ultramarine,
Prussian blue, cadmium yellow and orange for the last sun, ivory black for the wire and nothing
else. The red oxide is not on the palette; it is the ground. Turquoise only for the basket.

## What she is not

Not a painter of Malta, and she got cross when people at the festa said she was. No balconies, no
boats, no harbour. The pictures are about work that finishes every day and is undone the next
morning, and about a wind you cannot photograph.

## Mechanisms she owns

See `tools.py`. A roof she can stand on with her eye at any height and her head tipped up; a wire
between two posts that sags; washing that is a surface the wind holds, so she can ask any point of
it where it is and which way it faces; strokes that run down that surface from the pegs or round the
belly; and a sun she places behind a chosen sheet, whose light reaches each point of cloth only
through whatever stands in its way. For still days, washing that only hangs (`Hang`): swags between the
pegs and folds that fall from them, and strokes that change colour along their length as they run
out of a fold into the light.
