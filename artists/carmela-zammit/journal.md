# Journal

After each picture. What it taught, what went into the kit, what is still wrong.

## 001. Majjistral, last sun

8,049 strokes. Nine goes at it before it stood up, and it is still not all there.

**What held.** The sun behind the third piece on the near line. Everything else on the line is
lit from behind by the same sun at the same height, so the eye goes along the wire and stops where
the light comes straight through. I did not decide where the bright patch goes; I decided which
sheet the sun is behind and the patch went where it had to. The doubled band at the top of each
sheet, where it is folded over the wire, comes out darker by itself, and so does the hem. The far
line works as I hoped: the bath towel and the tablecloth on it stand between the sun and the near
sheets, and the near sheets go grey exactly where they are behind them. The big sheet on the left
is nearly all in the towel's shadow, which is why it is the dull one, and I left it dull.

**What didn't.**
- First go, the washing hung square to me in a row, like a curtain. Three bands, sky, sheets, roof,
  and nothing to walk into. The lines now run away from me to the right, which gives the wire its
  slope and lets each piece turn a little more edge-on as it goes.
- I had the sun behind the tablecloth as well as the sheet, so the one place that should have been
  white was the dimmest. I moved the far line's pieces until the sun had a gap to come through.
- My strokes came out as fat little sausages and the red showed between all of them, sky and roof
  both. More paint, longer strokes, a first scrub of the sky colour over the whole top half before
  the real sky. The red now only shows in the sky as thin warm wisps, which at that hour is not
  wrong, but it is heavier at the top edge than it should be.
- Thick paint everywhere reads as chalk: every ridge catches the light and the whole thing goes pale
  and busy. Only the sun gets body now.
- The sun went in first as short thick dabs and looked like fish scales. Longer strokes, wetter,
  in rings, then a few strokes of nearly pure white in the middle.
- Hanging strokes left a fringe at every hem, like trouser legs. A pass along the hem, across, seals
  them. Same at the fold over the wire.
- I held the roof down by hand (`near_dark`) and the sky's top darker than it was, so the cotton
  is the light. That is a decision, not the weather.
- The basket was cut off by the bottom of the board twice before I moved it back.

**Still wrong.** The roof is a wide grey floor with a basket on it; it is quiet, which is right,
but it is too empty and the line where the wall meets the roof is too straight and too even. The
sun is bright but not yet hot: it reads as the lightest patch rather than as something you
cannot look at. The tablecloth's border should show through the third sheet as a darker band and
mostly doesn't. The dome is a cut-out.

**Into the kit.** `Camera` with pitch, so I can tip my head up. `Line`, a wire that sags. `Sheet`,
washing as a surface the wind holds: belly toward me, hem lifting and flaring, folds hanging from
each peg; it can tell me any point and which way it faces, and whether a ray to the sun passes
through it. `lay_cloth`, strokes that follow the cloth down from the pegs or across the hem.
`lay_screen` for sky and roof. `sheeting`, the support with its darns.

**Asked of the engine.** A stroke whose colour can change along its length; my hanging strokes run
from shadow into light and have to be one colour each, so I make them short and many. And a way to
lay a mask from more than one tape at once that also lets an outside and an inside combine (the far
wire had to go behind the near washing; I taped outside the near sheets, which works only because
nothing else needed taping at the time).

**Next.** Xlokk. The sirocco, sky the colour of dishwater, no sun to put behind anything, the sheets
heavy and barely moving. See if the cloth still holds the picture without the light doing the work.
