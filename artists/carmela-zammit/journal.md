# Journal

After each picture. What it taught, what went into the kit, what is still wrong.

## 001. Majjistral, last sun

8,049 strokes. Nine goes at it before it stood up, and it is still not all there.

**What held.** The sun behind the third piece on the near line. Everything else on the line is
lit from behind by the same sun at the same height, so the eye goes along the wire and stops where
the light comes straight through. I did not decide where the bright patch goes; I decided which
sheet the sun is behind and the patch went where it had to. The doubled band at the top of each
sheet, where it is folded over the wire, comes out darker by itself, and so does the hem. The far
line works as I hoped: the bath towel and the tablecloth on it stand between the sun and the near
sheets, and the near sheets go grey exactly where they are behind them. The big sheet on the left
is nearly all in the towel's shadow, which is why it is the dull one, and I left it dull.

**What didn't.**
- First go, the washing hung square to me in a row, like a curtain. Three bands, sky, sheets, roof,
  and nothing to walk into. The lines now run away from me to the right, which gives the wire its
  slope and lets each piece turn a little more edge-on as it goes.
- I had the sun behind the tablecloth as well as the sheet, so the one place that should have been
  white was the dimmest. I moved the far line's pieces until the sun had a gap to come through.
- My strokes came out as fat little sausages and the red showed between all of them, sky and roof
  both. More paint, longer strokes, a first scrub of the sky colour over the whole top half before
  the real sky. The red now only shows in the sky as thin warm wisps, which at that hour is not
  wrong, but it is heavier at the top edge than it should be.
- Thick paint everywhere reads as chalk: every ridge catches the light and the whole thing goes pale
  and busy. Only the sun gets body now.
- The sun went in first as short thick dabs and looked like fish scales. Longer strokes, wetter,
  in rings, then a few strokes of nearly pure white in the middle.
- Hanging strokes left a fringe at every hem, like trouser legs. A pass along the hem, across, seals
  them. Same at the fold over the wire.
- I held the roof down by hand (`near_dark`) and the sky's top darker than it was, so the cotton
  is the light. That is a decision, not the weather.
- The basket was cut off by the bottom of the board twice before I moved it back.

**Still wrong.** The roof is a wide grey floor with a basket on it; it is quiet, which is right,
but it is too empty and the line where the wall meets the roof is too straight and too even. The
sun is bright but not yet hot: it reads as the lightest patch rather than as something you
cannot look at. The tablecloth's border should show through the third sheet as a darker band and
mostly doesn't. The dome is a cut-out.

**Into the kit.** `Camera` with pitch, so I can tip my head up. `Line`, a wire that sags. `Sheet`,
washing as a surface the wind holds: belly toward me, hem lifting and flaring, folds hanging from
each peg; it can tell me any point and which way it faces, and whether a ray to the sun passes
through it. `lay_cloth`, strokes that follow the cloth down from the pegs or across the hem.
`lay_screen` for sky and roof. `sheeting`, the support with its darns.

**Asked of the engine.** A stroke whose colour can change along its length; my hanging strokes run
from shadow into light and have to be one colour each, so I make them short and many. And a way to
lay a mask from more than one tape at once that also lets an outside and an inside combine (the far
wire had to go behind the near washing; I taped outside the near sheets, which works only because
nothing else needed taping at the time).

**Next.** Xlokk. The sirocco, sky the colour of dishwater, no sun to put behind anything, the sheets
heavy and barely moving. See if the cloth still holds the picture without the light doing the work.

## 002. Xlokk, nothing dries

34,023 strokes. Eleven goes. The cloth holds it, just; the roof still doesn't.

**What held.** Without a sun there is nothing to decide but the folds, so the picture is the near
sheet on the right. Wet cotton does not ripple, it drops: a swag between each pair of pegs and one
deep fold from the middle of every swag down to the hem, two or three smaller ones beside it, and
nothing finer. Once I painted that and only that, the sheet got heavy. The water has gone to the
hem, so the bottom of every piece is greyer than the top, and that does more for "nothing dries"
than anything else in it. The sky is lamp enough if the light comes from in front of the cloth
and a little to the right: every fold then has a side that sees more of it and a side that sees
less, and the inside of a fold goes the colder grey. Under the line the screed is still wet from
the morning, and the near sheet shows in it upside down, dragged toward me. Qormi went in while
the sky was wet, with a soft brush, and it sank into it; it is not a cut-out any more, it is
nearly not there, which is right in a xlokk.

**Colour along a stroke.** This is the picture it was for. Every hanging stroke now carries the
cloth from the shadow at the peg down into the light of the open fold and on into the wet grey at
the hem, so a fold is one stroke instead of six stacked dabs that each had to agree with the next.
In 001 the folds were bands of separate marks; here they are continuous and you read the depth of
the fold, not the brushwork. Same for the hem and the band over the wire, laid across. The wet
patch under the line is the same trick sideways: each stroke runs from dry screed into reflection.

**What didn't.**
- First go had 70% sky, the washing small and far off, and the red coming through the sky in
  blotches like pink candyfloss. Brought her closer, a longer lens, the horizon lower, the sky
  covered properly; the red now shows only in a few thin places, where at least it is the dust.
- I had the sky's brightest part to the side of the cloth, behind its plane, so only the folds
  that turned sharply toward it caught any light and every far sheet broke out in white flecks
  like something torn. Moving the light in front of the cloth fixed it at once.
- The folds came out as fine streaks on the distant pieces. A brush scaled to the distance and
  less relief far off.
- Drip marks on the roof read as footprints, then as tyre tracks. Took them out and let the wet
  floor show it instead, only under the line.
- The basket came out mint green and pulled the whole picture to it. Knocked down with sienna
  and white. Its heap of wet sheets looked like a cake lid, then like a cake; now it is a lumpy
  top with three folded edges.
- The wall line: a side wall now runs away on the left into a corner, so the parapet breaks.

**Still wrong.** The floor between the wet strip and the basket is still a flat tan field. The
mid-line sheets are less certain than the near one; their hems are ragged where the brush ran past
the tape. The basket is still the only colour in it and the eye goes there second, before the
line. The far line is nearly all hidden and is not doing anything. The sky is honest but dull to
look at for a third of the board.

**Into the kit.** `Hang`, washing with no wind in it: swags between pegs, folds tied to where the
pegs are, a `crease(u, v)` that says how far into a fold a point is, so shade can darken the
inside. `lay_cloth(..., run=True)`, which asks for a colour at every point along the stroke.

**Asked of the engine.** A wet floor wants to reflect what stands on it; I traced the reflected
ray by hand and mixed it into each stroke, which works but is slow and lives in one painting.
Not asking for it yet; if it comes up again it belongs in my kit first. What I would like from
the engine is a tape that can be feathered on one side only: a hem wants a hard edge at the
bottom and a soft one where the fold strokes come down into it.

**Next.** The roof is still the problem in both pictures. Next time paint the roof as the subject
and the washing at the edge of it: Gregale, the north-easter, cold and bright, everything on the
line going one way and a basket blown over.

## 003. Grigal, the basket over

11,316 strokes. About a dozen goes. The first one where the roof is the picture and not the floor
under it.

**What held.** I turned my eyes down. The board is upright for once, and the line runs along the
top of it with the sheets going out of the picture; what I painted is the roof. A January sun low
behind the line throws the washing's shadow four, five metres toward me, and the shadow is where
the wind is: every piece pulled the same way, the hems torn ragged, the pillowcase a flag. I laid
the whole roof in as if nothing were on the line, warm, then laid the shadows on top, cut to
where the light cuts them and brushed the way the sun throws them. They came out blue with warm
in them, because a sheet is not a wall: one thickness of cotton lets a fifth of the sun through,
and the hems almost none. Between two shadows the sun gets through in thin streaks and they run
straight at you. The sheet the wind is taking out of the basket lies across the top of the shadow,
the one thing on the roof the sun hits square, and that is where the thick paint went. The prop's
shadow runs across it in the cloth's own blue, and I like that more than anything else in it.

**What didn't.**
- I had the wind blowing toward me first, which is a majjistral if I am facing the sun. Turned it
  round so it comes over my right shoulder; then the sheets swing away and their shadows open up.
- The roof was a khaki desert and the shadows grey; the sun has to be twice what I thought before
  the screed looks lit, and the shadows need the sky in them, not the sun.
- The sheet on the ground was a magic carpet, then foil, then a canoe, then a leaf. It took a
  broader far end with two real corners, only one lifting, rucks few and wide, a hem round it, and
  the shaded side warmed by the roof under it.
- The basket on its side read as a tin, then a drum. Turning its mouth toward me so the dark inside
  shows, and two rows of slots, made it a basket.
- I tried sun spots through the basket's slots on the roof. They came out as thrown rice. Out.
- Long lines on the roof that started off the board came back with a dry brush: the joints and the
  prop's shadow were not there. Start them at the edge.

**Still wrong.** The near roof, the bottom third on the right, is a field of warm paint with two pegs
in it; it is the open sun the shadows need, but it is the weakest passage and the dullest paint.
The basket is still the darkest, most saturated thing and it sits right beside the brightest, so
the two pull together. The washing at the top is all one cream; it should glow more at the edges.
The far roof between the wall's shadow and the washing's is soft dabs that say nothing.

**Into the kit.** `Gust`, washing in a hard wind: pegged along the top, the rest swung out the
wind's way, more the further from the pegs, waves running down it and the free corners going over.
`cast`, the shadow anything throws on the roof: one thickness, two, and the product where they
cross, from any surface the kit can ask a point of. `lay_field`, roof strokes that change colour
along their length as they run into a shadow and out.

**Changed.** The sun can be off the top of the board now, not only behind a sheet, and the thick goes
where it lands hardest on cloth. Shadows are laid over a roof painted all in sun. Persona updated.

**Asked of the engine.** A stroke that starts off the board should arrive with its paint: bristles
lose load every step they are off the edge, so a long line begun outside the board is dry by the
time it gets on. I clip my lines now. And a tape that can be cut from a soft field with a threshold
and a hard edge in one go; I threshold by hand.

**Next.** A night. The roof lit only from the stairwell door, the washing left out by mistake, and
the light coming the other way through it for once: from in front, from below.

## 004. Bonaċċa, left out

14,615 strokes. About fourteen goes. August, a dead calm, and the washing still out at eleven at
night because nobody went up for it. The first picture with no sun in it at all.

**What held.** The bulb on the stairs, off the board on the left, coming up through the door of the
stair-head at the height of my hip. Everything I had worked out with the sun behind the cloth I had
to turn round: the light is on my side now and lands on the face of the sheet I can see, so I lay
the whole line in the night first and then put the bulb on it, and it dies along the line the way it
does in life, the near sheet nearly white and the tablecloth at the far end only just there. A bulb
is close, so the shadows are big: the near sheet's stands up on the house wall behind three times
its size, and the whole line is up there again with its wire and its pegs, the wire's shadow running
downhill across the wall where the real wire is nearly level. Between the pegs the cloth's shadow
sags below the wire's, which is right and which I would not have thought of. The door cuts the light
too: the left of the wall is only the night, and the screed in front of me has the jamb's edge
running across it on a slant. I put the basket by the door, empty, with its shadow going out
toward the line it was meant to take the washing off. That is the picture's story and it cost one
ellipse.

**What didn't.**
- First go the bulb was off to the left and far back and the line ran square across the board: a
  fence of sheets, a wall, a floor, a sky, four bands. I lowered my eye (I sat on the low wall), ran
  the line away from me to a hook in the house wall, and brought the bulb close to the first sheet.
- With the bulb well to the left the shadows went off to the right and left the middle of the wall
  empty. With it nearer me they stand up behind the sheets that throw them.
- The bulb right down the stairs, below the roof, lights the cloth from underneath and nothing else:
  the screed went black and the shadows went up into the sky. Hip height lets the floor have a pool.
- The stone was as bright as the cotton. At night the eye gives everything to the white; I hold the
  roof at half and the wall at a little more.
- Shadow strokes on the wall laid the way the bulb throws them fan out from one point beside the
  door and looked like a palm tree. On a wall the shadow of hanging cloth hangs: I brush it down.
- The jamb's shadow laid in short strokes left drips and arches at every stroke end. Long strokes
  begun off the board, along the courses, stopped by the tape at the jamb, and it is a wall again.
- The folds read as bars, then as nothing. Less light bounced inside the folds, deeper folds on the
  near sheets, and the thick strokes put in wet and broad: thin and dry they stood up like grass.
- The far post and its shadow made a bracket. Out; the line goes to a hook in the wall.

**Still wrong.** The upper right of the wall is a big field of warm stone with a drainpipe in it, and
it is the weakest passage now that the floor has the basket. The jamb's shadow on the left of the
wall is flat and its edge glows orange where the penumbra is, which is true and looks like a lit
pilaster. The sheets are lit as evenly at the pegs as at the hem; I said "from below" and the bulb at
hip height only gives it to the top half, and not so you would see. The outside of the basket is one
even green. Red comes through the floor in patches that read as rust, not dust.

**Into the kit.** `Bulb`, a point of light seen only through a doorway: it falls off with the
square of the distance and the jambs cut it, soft by how big the bulb is. `cast_point`, the shadow a
point light lays on any plane, floor or wall, through one thickness of cotton or two. `thrown`, the
way a point light throws shadows across a plane, to brush them by. `on_plane`, the point on a plane
behind a point on the board.

**Changed.** She paints nights now and then, and the bulb is never painted any more than the sun is.
Under a bulb the shadows fan out on the floor and stand up on a wall, and on a wall she brushes them
the way the cloth hangs. Persona updated.

**Asked of the engine.** A colour given as a field over the board, read where the stroke passes,
the way pressure can be: every stroke on the roof and wall asks a Python function for its colour at
six points, and most of the time goes there. And again, a tape cut from a soft field at a threshold
with a hard edge in one go; I still clip and multiply by hand.

**Next.** Tramuntana, and the line empty: the morning after the laundry stopped sending linen up,
only the pegs left on the wire and their shadows on the screed. See whether the roof holds a picture
with no cloth on it at all.
